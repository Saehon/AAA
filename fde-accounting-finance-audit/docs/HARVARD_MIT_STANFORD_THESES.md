### Prioritization

**Immediately actionable:** MIT Downing (data integration before AI adoption); MIT South (verifiable AI and multi-agent authorization); MIT Sert and Li (hallucinations and uncertainty); Harvard Long (agent reliability and costly tail events); Harvard Sarkar (look-ahead leakage); Stanford Mark Zhao (secure data infrastructure) and Eric Mitchell (useful AI systems). Their lessons lead to a production readiness checklist—not claims that the FDE demo already implements these capabilities.

**Domain-adjacent methods:** MIT Zijie Zhao (financial RAG and decision risk), MIT Daqqah (complex enterprise document extraction), Harvard Shi (economic outcomes of disclosure/information access), Harvard Shavit (verification incentives), Stanford Murty (interaction learning), Stanford Hancheng Cao (future of work), Stanford Moss (safe planning). These inform a future empirical and systems research program; they do not independently certify accounting controls.

### Citation and research integrity

- Record author, exact thesis title, university, year, degree, original institutional record and permitted use.
- Distinguish published articles from working papers, doctoral theses, masters theses and class projects.
- Do not claim university endorsement of this software or that the thesis author collaborated on this project.
- Verify links and any associated code license before adapting an algorithm.
- If a dissertation develops a method that was later peer-reviewed, cite the **separate journal article** when the claim concerns peer-reviewed evidence, noting that the dissertation is background methodology.
- For an actual FT50 submission, complete an additional systematic novelty and replication search; this selection is targeted, not exhaustive.
