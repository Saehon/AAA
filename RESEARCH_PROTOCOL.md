# NAAIL Scientific Discovery Protocol for AAA

AAA uses the following research protocol for new studies and substantive upgrades.

## 1. Co-Scientist hypothesis arena
Generate multiple competing hypotheses. Critique, rank, debate, and improve them before empirical testing. Retain rejected hypotheses and reasons for rejection.

## 2. ERA-style empirical conversion
Translate each surviving hypothesis into an explicit empirical object containing data sources, variables, estimands, identification strategy, executable code, evaluation metrics, robustness tests, and replication requirements.

## 3. AlphaEvolve-inspired computational discovery
Compare and evolve candidate models, algorithms, constructs, prompts, estimators, and specifications against frozen scientific fitness criteria. Never optimize for statistical significance alone.

## 4. AlphaFold-inspired latent-structure discovery
Search for hidden factors, regimes, networks, clusters, representations, causal structures, or mechanisms in high-dimensional accounting and audit data. Require economic/accounting interpretation and external validation.

## 5. AI-to-AI adversarial review
Use independent critic, defender, replicator, and evaluator roles where appropriate. Agent agreement is not treated as evidence of truth.

## 6. Science One-style Chain-of-Evidence
Maintain traceability across:

Research question → theory → literature → data → variable construction → model → identification → results → robustness → replication → interpretation → human decision.

## 7. DAG governance
Do not permit a research claim when required theory, data, identification, code, provenance, or validation dependencies are incomplete.

## 8. Mirendil-inspired closed-loop R&D
Use a controlled propose → pretest → execute → evaluate → critique → mutate → retest cycle while retaining failure memory and preserving scientific gates.

## 9. Mandatory discovery gates
No result may be described as a scientific discovery until it has passed, where applicable:

- literature validation and contradictory-evidence search;
- data provenance and leakage review;
- construct validation;
- identification review;
- robustness and falsification;
- temporal/out-of-sample validation;
- reproducibility and replication;
- AI-to-AI adversarial review;
- Chain-of-Evidence completion;
- human approval.
